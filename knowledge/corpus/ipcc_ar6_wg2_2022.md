# IPCC Sixth Assessment Report, Working Group II - Impacts, Adaptation and Vulnerability (2022)

## Observed impacts on ecosystems
Climate change has caused substantial damage and increasingly irreversible losses in terrestrial, freshwater and coastal ecosystems. Approximately half of the species assessed globally have shifted poleward or to higher elevations. The magnitude and rate of these shifts vary between taxa, which breaks previously synchronised interactions such as those between plants and their pollinators.

## Water availability and species survival
Increases in the frequency and intensity of drought have driven tree mortality and vegetation dieback in multiple regions. Water availability acts as a primary control on species persistence in water-limited systems: as dry spells lengthen, species without access to deeper or more persistent moisture stores are lost first, so community composition shifts toward drought-tolerant taxa and overall richness declines.

## Compound and interacting risks
Risks are compounding. Climate impacts interact with non-climatic drivers such as habitat fragmentation, pollution and land-use intensification, and the combined effect is frequently greater than the sum of the individual pressures. A fragmented population is less able to track a shifting climate envelope because dispersal routes are absent, so habitat connectivity determines whether species can respond to climate change at all.

## Adaptation and ecosystem-based approaches
Ecosystem-based adaptation, including the maintenance and restoration of natural and semi-natural habitat, can reduce climate risk while delivering biodiversity and livelihood co-benefits. Effective adaptation for biodiversity includes maintaining connectivity between habitat patches, protecting climate refugia, and diversifying production systems.

## Microclimate buffering
Vegetation structure moderates the microclimate experienced by organisms at ground level. Canopy cover reduces maximum surface temperatures and lowers vapour pressure deficit beneath it, creating conditions that are measurably cooler and moister than in adjacent open ground. This buffering allows species to persist locally under regional warming that would otherwise exceed their tolerance, which is a specific mechanism by which structural habitat interventions deliver climate adaptation rather than only biodiversity benefit.

## Limits to adaptation
Soft and hard adaptation limits are being approached in some systems. Where warming exceeds species' physiological tolerance and no refugia or dispersal routes exist, local loss occurs regardless of management. Adaptation measures are therefore more effective when deployed before thresholds are crossed.
