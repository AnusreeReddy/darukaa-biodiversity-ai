# A meta-analysis of global cropland soil carbon changes due to cover cropping

## Scope
A global synthesis of paired cropland observations testing how cover cropping alters soil organic carbon (SOC) stocks, and which environmental and management factors modulate the response.

## Mean effect
Across all studies the mean rate of carbon sequestration from cover cropping was 0.56 Mg per hectare per year. Scaled hypothetically to 15% of current global cropland, this corresponds to 0.16 +/- 0.06 Pg of carbon sequestered per year, roughly 1-2% of current fossil fuel emissions.

## Soil texture strongly controls the response
Medium-textured soils held the highest absolute SOC stocks, with means of 39 Mg per hectare under cover crops and 37 Mg per hectare without. However, fine-textured soils showed the greatest relative increase after cover crops were introduced, with a mean change of 39.5%. Coarse-textured soils gained 11.4% and medium-textured soils 10.3%. Clay content therefore matters: fine-textured soils have greater mineral surface area available to stabilise new organic carbon, so the same biomass input converts into a larger and more persistent stock.

## Other moderators
SOC change was also affected by mean annual temperature, the number of years since cover crops were adopted, latitude, and the initial SOC concentration. Sites starting from low initial SOC had more headroom and showed larger relative gains. Warmer sites lose a greater fraction of new inputs to mineralisation, so sustained annual input is required to hold the gain.

## Co-benefits observed
Increases in SOC were correlated with decreased runoff and erosion, and with increased mineralisable carbon, mineralisable nitrogen and total soil nitrogen. These are the properties that connect soil carbon to the wider ecosystem: mineralisable carbon is the substrate for the microbial community, and reduced runoff means more of the incident rainfall is retained in the profile rather than lost as overland flow.

## Uncertainty
Reported sequestration rates vary widely between syntheses, from roughly 0.2 to 0.9 Mg per hectare per year depending on depth sampled, climate, and cover crop biomass. Any site-level projection should be treated as a range rather than a point estimate.
