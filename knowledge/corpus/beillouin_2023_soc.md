# A global meta-analysis of soil organic carbon in the Anthropocene

## Scope
A global meta-analysis quantifying how land-use change and land-management practices alter soil organic carbon (SOC) stocks.

## Land-use change losses
Conversion of forest to cropland causes large SOC losses, but the magnitude depends strongly on what the land is converted to. Croplands cultivated with annual crops lost approximately 32% of SOC (CI: -38% to -26%). Where converted land was cultivated under agroforestry practices the loss was only about 12% (CI: -19% to -4.9%), and under perennial crops about 7.2% (CI: -18% to +4.3%, i.e. not significantly different from zero).

This is one of the most decision-relevant findings in the literature: the carbon penalty of clearing forest is not fixed. It is roughly two-thirds smaller if the replacement system retains woody perennial cover. Where deforestation has already occurred, re-introducing woody perennials is the mechanism by which part of that loss is recovered.

## Management practices that increase SOC
Agroforestry significantly increased SOC in croplands by +20% (CI: +17% to +23%). This is measured against cropland controls, so it represents the gain available from converting an existing annual cropping system to one incorporating trees, rather than against a forest baseline.

## Implication for degraded land
Because SOC losses from annual cropping are large and agroforestry conversion recovers a substantial share, sites that are simultaneously low in SOC and recently deforested have the largest technical headroom for improvement. The same intervention addresses both the carbon deficit and the structural habitat deficit created by tree removal.
