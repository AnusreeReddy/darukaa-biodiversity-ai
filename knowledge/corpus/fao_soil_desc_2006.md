# FAO - Guidelines for Soil Description, 4th edition (2006)

## Soil reaction (pH) classes
The guidelines define standard descriptive classes for soil reaction measured in water, used for consistent field description and interpretation:
- Ultra acidic: below 3.5
- Extremely acidic: 3.5 to 4.5
- Strongly acidic: 4.5 to 5.5
- Moderately acidic: 5.5 to 6.5
- Neutral: 6.5 to 7.5
- Moderately alkaline: 7.5 to 8.5
- Strongly alkaline: above 8.5

## Agronomic significance of the classes
Nutrient availability varies systematically across these classes. Phosphorus availability peaks near neutrality and declines in both directions, being fixed by aluminium and iron under acid conditions and by calcium under alkaline conditions. Micronutrient cations such as iron, manganese and zinc become less available as pH rises above neutral, producing chlorosis on calcareous soils. Below pH 5.5, aluminium becomes increasingly soluble and is directly toxic to root apical meristems, restricting rooting depth and therefore the volume of soil from which water can be drawn.

## Soil description context
The guidelines also standardise the recording of soil colour, texture, structure, consistency, root abundance, biological activity and surface features. Recording biological features such as earthworm channels, faunal casts and rooting density alongside chemical measurements is recommended because these respond faster to management change than bulk chemical properties.

## Field use
Because pH varies with depth, season and sampling method, a single value should be interpreted as indicative rather than definitive, and comparisons over time require consistent sampling depth and method.
