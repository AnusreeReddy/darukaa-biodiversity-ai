# IPBES Global Assessment Report on Biodiversity and Ecosystem Services, 2019 - Summary for Policymakers

## State of nature
Nature is declining globally at rates unprecedented in human history. Around 1 million animal and plant species are threatened with extinction, many within decades. The average abundance of native species in most major terrestrial biomes has fallen by at least 20%, mostly since 1900.

## Direct drivers, ranked
The assessment ranks the direct drivers of biodiversity loss, in descending order of global relative impact on terrestrial systems:
1. Changes in land and sea use (habitat conversion and land-use intensification)
2. Direct exploitation of organisms
3. Climate change
4. Pollution
5. Invasive alien species

This ranking is operationally important. Where habitat has been converted or simplified, land-use change is usually the dominant constraint, and interventions that restore habitat structure address the first-ranked driver directly. Where chemical loading is heavy, pollution acts as a separate and additive pressure that habitat restoration alone will not remove.

## Land-use change
More than a third of the world's land surface and nearly 75% of freshwater resources are devoted to crop or livestock production. Three-quarters of the terrestrial environment has been significantly altered by human actions. Approximately 85% of wetlands present in 1700 had been lost by 2000, a faster rate of loss than for forests.

## Land degradation and productivity
Land degradation has reduced the productivity of 23% of the global land surface. Between US$235 billion and US$577 billion in annual global crop output is at risk as a result of pollinator loss.

## Habitat fragmentation and connectivity
Habitat loss is compounded by fragmentation. Remaining patches that are small and isolated support fewer species than the same total area held in connected units, because local extinctions are not offset by recolonisation. Restoring connectivity between remnant patches is therefore a distinct intervention from increasing total habitat area, and the two are not substitutes.

## Response
The assessment concludes that goals for conserving and sustainably using nature cannot be met by current trajectories, and that transformative change is required. Among land-based responses it highlights ecological restoration, agroecological approaches, and the maintenance of landscape-scale habitat heterogeneity.
