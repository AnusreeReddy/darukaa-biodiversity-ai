# Carbon sequestration in agricultural soils via cultivation of cover crops - A meta-analysis

## Scope and method
This meta-analysis compiled 33 studies covering 47 sites and 147 plots that compared arable rotations with and without cover crops. A pedotransfer function was used to estimate bulk density so that soil organic carbon (SOC) concentrations could be converted into stocks, making sites comparable. Observation periods extended up to 54 years.

## Headline result
SOC stock change was a linear function of time since cover crops were introduced. The mean annual sequestration rate was 0.32 +/- 0.08 Mg C per hectare per year over a mean sampling depth of 22 cm. Time since introduction explained part of the variance (R2 = 0.19), with elevation and sampling depth improving model fit.

## Saturation behaviour
No saturation was visible within the observed period. Using the RothC carbon turnover model with an annual carbon input of 1.87 Mg C per hectare, a new steady state was predicted only after approximately 155 years, with a total mean SOC stock accumulation of 16.7 +/- 1.5 Mg C per hectare at 22 cm depth. The practical implication is that cover cropping produces incremental but persistent gains: meaningful change in SOC concentration is measurable over several years, not within a single season.

## Global significance
Scaling the observed rate gave a potential global SOC sequestration of 0.12 +/- 0.03 Pg C per year, equivalent to roughly 8% of direct annual greenhouse gas emissions from agriculture. Altered nitrous oxide emissions and albedo changes from cover crop cultivation were not included in this budget.

## Interpretation for land management
Because the gain is driven by additional carbon input rather than by reduced tillage alone, the biomass productivity of the cover crop is the controlling variable. Cover crops that establish quickly and produce substantial above- and below-ground biomass deliver the upper end of the range. Under favourable conditions the contribution exceeds that attributable to no-till alone, and the two practices are additive.
