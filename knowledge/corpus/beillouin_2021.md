# Positive but variable effects of crop diversification on biodiversity and ecosystem services

## Scope and method
A second-order synthesis compiling 95 meta-analyses, which together integrate 5,156 experiments conducted over 84 experimental years, representing more than 54,500 paired observations across 120 crop species in 85 countries. Diversification strategies assessed include agroforestry, intercropping, crop rotation, cover crops, variety mixtures and landscape heterogeneity.

## Overall effects
Taken together, crop diversification strategies improved associated biodiversity by a median of +24% (95% CI: 15% to 33%), where associated biodiversity means the non-cultivated plants and animals living within the agroecosystem. Crop production rose by a median of +14% (CI: 8% to 20%). Regulating and supporting services also improved: water quality +51% (CI: 3% to 123%), pest and disease control +63% (CI: 31% to 103%), and soil quality +11% (CI: 7% to 16%).

## Effects differ sharply between strategies
The choice of diversification strategy matters more than the decision to diversify in the abstract. Reported effects on associated biodiversity were:
- Agroforestry: +61% (CI: 25% to 105%) higher biodiversity than monoculture systems.
- Crop rotation: +37% (CI: 16% to 62%).
- Cover crops: +21% (CI: 17% to 25%).
- Intercropping: +7% (CI: 3% to 12%).
- Variety mixtures: approximately +2%.

Agroforestry is therefore the single most effective diversification strategy for associated biodiversity by a wide margin, because it adds vertical structure and perennial resources rather than only temporal or genetic variation.

## Why the mechanisms differ
Strategies that introduce perennial woody structure change the habitat template itself: they provide year-round floral and nesting resources, microclimate buffering, and physical refugia that annual-crop diversification cannot supply. Rotations and cover crops act mainly through temporal resource continuity and soil biological change. This explains the ordering of effect sizes and means that a recommendation should match the limiting factor: if structural habitat is absent, temporal diversification alone will under-deliver.

## Variability and caution
There was substantial variability between strategies for each individual ecosystem service, and confidence intervals are wide for several outcomes, notably water quality. Effects are context-dependent, and the synthesis reports medians rather than guaranteed site-level outcomes.
