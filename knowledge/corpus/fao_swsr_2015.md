# FAO and ITPS - Status of the World's Soil Resources, 2015

## Principal soil threats
The report identifies the major global threats to soil function, including soil erosion, loss of soil organic carbon, nutrient imbalance, soil acidification, soil contamination, waterlogging, soil compaction, soil sealing, salinisation and loss of soil biodiversity. Loss of soil organic carbon is identified as one of the most significant and widespread.

## Soil organic carbon and soil function
Soil organic carbon underpins several soil functions at once. It contributes to aggregate stability, which governs resistance to erosion and infiltration capacity; it supplies the energy substrate for the soil microbial and faunal community; it holds and releases nutrients through cation exchange and mineralisation; and it increases plant-available water capacity. A decline in SOC therefore degrades physical, chemical and biological soil function simultaneously rather than any one of them in isolation.

## Soil biodiversity
Soils host a very large share of terrestrial biodiversity. Soil organisms drive nutrient cycling, decomposition, soil structure formation and the regulation of pests and diseases. Loss of soil organic matter reduces the habitat and energy supply available to this community, so soil biodiversity decline is typically a consequence of carbon depletion rather than an independent process.

## Soil acidification
Acidification reduces the availability of phosphorus and molybdenum and mobilises aluminium and manganese into toxic soluble forms. Strongly acidic conditions impair root growth and inhibit rhizobial symbiosis, which is why legume establishment often fails on acid soils unless pH is corrected first. Acidification is driven by ammonium-based fertiliser use, removal of basic cations in harvested product, and leaching under high rainfall.

## Salinisation and sodicity
Salt-affected soils occur predominantly in arid and semi-arid regions, where evaporation exceeds precipitation and irrigation without adequate drainage concentrates salts in the root zone. Sodicity degrades soil structure by dispersing clay, which collapses pore space and reduces infiltration.

## Management response
The report concludes that the majority of soil threats can be addressed with existing knowledge through practices that maintain soil cover, increase organic inputs, diversify rotations, minimise mechanical disturbance and manage nutrients according to crop demand.
